Overview

Why kinematics still matters

Planning a path from A to B may look solved, but pick-and-place tasks in cluttered environments still demand fast, safe replanning when objects move unexpectedly. The difficulty is often geometric: robot motion is inherently limited by kinematics.

This course develops an analytical, intuition-driven understanding of robot kinematics, singularity visualization, and constraint-aware motion planning with joint limits, collision avoidance, and singularity robustness.

Syllabus

From models to robust motion

The course is hands-on and project-oriented, with practical sessions in realistic robot simulations.

Robot modeling for planning

Frames, DH parameters, URDF, joint limits, and collision geometry.

Analytical kinematics

Forward and inverse kinematics, multiple IK solutions, and solution continuation along a path.

Singularity analysis

Visualization and interpretation of possible motions near singular configurations.

Differential kinematics

Jacobians as tools for conditioning metrics, interpretation, and robustness analysis.

Constraint-aware planning

Joint limits, singularity-aware planning, and execution in simulation.

Advanced glimpses

Screw-theory formulation and an introductory view of Conformal Geometric Algebra.

Final project

An end-to-end kinematics-grounded planning pipeline with evaluation and a concise report.

Exercises

Five exercises, one robot kinematics workflow

The exercises develop practical proficiency in robot-model inspection, forward and inverse kinematics, redundancy analysis, and the execution and evaluation of kinematically feasible motion-planning trajectories.

01

14 September · 10h15–12h00

Robot visualization

Load a URDF and its geometry, inspect the robot structure, and visualize link, joint, and reference frames. This module builds the geometric intuition needed to read a robot model before doing any kinematic computation.

Open exercise → 02

14 September · 10h15–12h00

Joint commands & forward kinematics

Command the robot through sliders, CSV trajectories, or the Python WebSocket interface, and observe the resulting end-effector pose. The module connects joint coordinates directly to forward kinematics and robot motion.

Open exercise → 03

14 September · 16h15–18h00

Inverse kinematics

Prescribe an end-effector pose and compare multiple inverse-kinematic configurations of the same robot. Solutions can be displayed individually or together, making the multiplicity of inverse kinematics visible rather than merely numerical.

Open exercise → 04

16 September · 10h15–12h00

Redundancy & self-motion

Capture a fixed end-effector pose and vary the redundancy parameter while the remaining joints compensate. A reference robot, animated ghost, self-motion trace, and draggable obstacle with live clearance make null-space motion tangible.

Open exercise → 05

16 September · 16h15–18h00

Motion planning & execution

Load a desired workspace pose path and a seven-joint trajectory, then animate the robot while comparing the prescribed Cartesian path with the forward-kinematic trace produced by the joint motion.

Assessment

Evaluation

Project teams, assessment weighting, submission deadlines, and report and presentation requirements.

How is the project organised?

The project will be completed in groups of two students.

How will the course be evaluated?

The final evaluation is based on the project report (50%) and the oral presentation (50%).

When is the project report due?

Submit the project report by 16 November 2026 at 18h00. A one-hour grace period is allowed, until 19h00 on 16 November. Submissions after this grace period will be penalized 1 point for lateness.

What are the project report requirements?

The report must be no more than 10 pages, with a single-column layout, 12 pt font, and 1-inch margins.

When is the oral presentation, and how is it organised?

Oral presentations take place on 25 November 2026. Each team has 20 minutes: 10 minutes presenting followed by 10 minutes of oral questions.

Both students must present for equal time: 5 minutes per student during the presentation.

When should we upload the presentation slides?

Upload your slides on Moddle one day before the oral presentation, on 24 November 2026.

Structure and content

How many credits and contact hours does the course have?

ENG-654 is worth 4 ECTS credits and comprises 56 contact hours.

How is the course workload divided?

The taught component comprises 32 hours split across four days. The remaining 24 hours are dedicated to the course project.

What topics do Part I and Part II cover?

The course is divided into two parts: Part I covers serial robots, while Part II covers parallel robots.
