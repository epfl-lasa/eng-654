Learning objective

Which geometric property makes analytical partitioning possible?

The axes of joints 4–6 intersect at one wrist point. Joints 1–3 place that point; joints 4–6 supply the residual orientation.

Partition at the wrist

Remove the complete tool transform before solving position

Let the desired tool pose be \({}^0T_t=[R_d,p_d]\), and let the constant wrist-to-tool transform from the model be \({}^wT_t=[R_{wt},p_{wt}]\).

\[ {}^0T_w={}^0T_t({}^wT_t)^{-1},\qquad R_w=R_dR_{wt}^{\top},\qquad p_w=p_d-R_dR_{wt}^{\top}p_{wt} \]

Evidence to show

  • Identify the wrist intersection in the course model.
  • Write the exact fixed transforms between joint 3, the wrist frame, and the tool.
  • Verify that joints 4–6 do not move \(p_w\).
  • Keep position and orientation in the same base-frame convention.
The shortcut \(p_w=p_d-\ell R_d\hat z\) is valid only when the wrist-to-tool transform is a pure offset of length \(\ell\) along that specified tool axis. Use the full transform for the custom robot.

Position IK decision tree

Check \(a_1=0\) before choosing the shoulder equations

Intersecting first axes: \(a_1=0\)

  • For \(\rho=\sqrt{x_w^2+y_w^2}>0\), reduce the target to a meridian plane and retain every valid shoulder direction implied by the remaining offsets.
  • For \(\rho=0\), \(q_1\) may be undetermined: treat it as a base-axis singular subcase, not one numerical angle.
  • Solve the remaining two-link geometry for both elbow signs whenever its discriminant is positive.
or

Offset first axes: \(a_1\neq0\)

  • Do not assume \(q_1=\operatorname{atan2}(y_w,x_w)\).
  • Derive the robot-specific shoulder equation \(A\sin q_1+B\cos q_1=C\).
  • When \(A^2+B^2>0\), test \(\Delta=A^2+B^2-C^2\): negative gives no solution, zero one tangent root, and positive two candidates.
  • If \(A=B=0\), solve separately: \(C=0\) leaves \(q_1\) arbitrary; \(C\ne0\) is infeasible.
After selecting a shoulder candidate, substitute it back into the original position equations and determine which elbow subcase the custom geometry produces.

Enumerate without losing solutions

Every algebraic division creates a subcase you must preserve

1

Write the three scalar equations \(p_w=f_p(q_1,q_2,q_3)\) from your own forward model.

2

Eliminate variables geometrically or with \(t_i=\tan(q_i/2)\); record every factor you divide by.

3

Solve the zero-factor cases separately, including roots at \(t_i=\infty\), which represent \(q_i=\pi\).

4

Back-substitute into the original equations, wrap angles only for comparison, and deduplicate modulo \(2\pi\).

Orientation IK

Each arm solution creates its own residual wrist rotation

\[{}^3R_w(q_4,q_5,q_6)=\bigl({}^0R_3(q_1,q_2,q_3)\bigr)^\top R_w\]

Match this matrix to the actual joint-axis sequence of the custom wrist. Do not import a Z–Y–Z formula unless the model has that sequence.

Regular wrist

Use robust atan2 expressions to recover the two flip-related solutions. Substitute both into the residual rotation.

Wrist singularity

When the middle-angle sine vanishes, axes 4 and 6 align. Their angles become coupled; report the family relation instead of dividing by zero.

Branch labels are bookkeeping, not proof. A shoulder/elbow/wrist label is valid only after the recovered joint vector reproduces the desired pose.

Validation campaign

Challenge the solver with poses whose answer you already know

Pose closure

\[e_p=\|p(q)-p_d\|_2,\quad e_R=\|\log(R_d^\top R(q))\|_2\]

Report tolerances and residuals for every returned branch.

Branch completeness

Generate targets from known random configurations. Verify that the source configuration is recovered modulo periodicity and that all returned roots are distinct.

Physical filtering

First enumerate mathematical roots; then apply joint limits. Keep rejected roots in the report so the loss of each branch is explainable.

Test regular targets, workspace-boundary targets, \(a_1\)-specific subcases, and wrist-singular orientations. A solver tested only on one friendly pose is not validated.
