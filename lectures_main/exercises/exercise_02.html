06 · Paden–Kahan subproblem 3

Remove θ₁ and θ₂ with a distance

Complete the preceding step to unlock this slide.

Paden–Kahan subproblem 3 finds the rotation that puts a point at a prescribed distance from another point. The first two arm axes meet at the shoulder, so their rotations preserve the shoulder-to-wrist distance. Use this distance to solve θ₃, then recover θ₂ and θ₁ by Cramer’s rule.

For a desired tool pose Td = [Rd, pd], the wrist point is pw = pd − 0.126 Rdez. The offset 0.126 m includes 0.081 m in the last joint and the 0.045 m fixed tool.

Coordinates from the shoulder

x = pw,x, y = pw,y, z = pw,z − H
ρ² = x² + y², R = ρ² + z²

H = 0.34 m; L = U = 0.40 m. Here R is a squared distance; rotation matrices always carry a subscript.

The reduced FK gives

A = L + U c₃
B = −U cos(φ) s₃, C = −U sin(φ) s₃
E = A s₂ + B c₂, z = A c₂ − B s₂
x = c₁E − s₁C, y = s₁E + c₁C

Write cᵢ = cos(θᵢ), sᵢ = sin(θᵢ). Expand x² + y² + z² and cancel the rotations. Build R first, then solve for c₃, both signs of s₃, and θ₃. sigma3 takes +1 and −1. Use ^ for powers and atan2(y,x) for quadrant-aware angles.

07 · Keep the projection sign

Why can E have two values?

Complete the preceding step to unlock this slide.

A signed distance, not a radius

E is the component of the wrist projection along the first link’s rotating radial direction. C is its perpendicular component.

[x, y]ᵀ = Rz,2D(θ₁) [E, C]ᵀ
ρ² = E² + C²

For each elbow sign, C = −U sin(φ)s₃ is known. Build both E values with sigmaE = ±1. Use rho for ρ or rho2 for ρ².

Why must both E signs be retained?

A negative radicand gives no real branch. A zero radicand merges the two E branches. The generic case has two elbow signs × two E signs.

08 · Back-substitute

Two small systems, two angles

Complete the preceding step to unlock this slide.

For each pair (σ₃, σE), evaluate A, B, C and E. Apply Cramer’s rule to recover the cosine and sine together, then use atan2.

Recover θ₂

B c₂ + A s₂ = E
A c₂ − B s₂ = z

Write the determinant of the 2 × 2 coefficient matrix and replace one column at a time.

Recover θ₁

E c₁ − C s₁ = x
C c₁ + E s₁ = y

A vanishing determinant needs a separate singular-case analysis. Do not silently divide by zero.

12 · Decide which branches are usable

Same tool pose, different joint configurations

Complete the preceding step to unlock this slide.

Select a branch to compare its robot configuration with the target. Every listed branch has passed the full FK check. Classify each one using all seven URDF joint limits.

Keep the two checks separate

A mathematical IK solution must reproduce the position and orientation. A physically allowed solution must also respect every joint limit.

Angles are reported in [−π, π]. None of these joints has a ±2π permitted interval, so wrapping a rejected angle by one turn cannot rescue it.

Joint limits alone do not check collisions or plan a safe motion between configurations.
