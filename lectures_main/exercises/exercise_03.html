From Joint Screws to IK Distribution

Learning objective

Build a Jacobian whose every column has a geometric meaning

What motion does joint \(i\) create at the wrist point when \(\dot q_i=1\)?

Answer in one common base frame, stack the six twists, and locate every configuration where those motions become linearly dependent.

Fix the coordinate convention

Transform every axis and origin into the base frame

\[ o_i={}^0p_{J_i},\qquad z_i={}^0R_{J_i}\hat a_i,\qquad p_w={}^0p_w \]

For each joint \(i\)

  • Compose the chain only up to the joint frame.
  • Transform the local URDF axis \(\hat a_i\) as a direction, not a point.
  • Use any point on the physical axis for \(o_i\).
  • Evaluate \(p_w\), \(o_i\), and \(z_i\) at the same configuration.
A link origin need not lie on its joint axis. A joint-frame origin does. Mixing local and base coordinates can produce plausible-looking columns that are physically meaningless.

Construct the six screws

One cross product supplies the translational block

\[ J_{w,i}(q)= \begin{bmatrix} z_i(q)\times\bigl(p_w(q)-o_i(q)\bigr)\\[2mm] z_i(q) \end{bmatrix},\qquad J_w=\begin{bmatrix}J_{w,1}&\cdots&J_{w,6}\end{bmatrix} \]

Interpret before simplifying

The upper three entries are wrist-point velocity per unit joint rate. The lower three entries are angular velocity per unit joint rate.

We use the convention \([v^\top\;\omega^\top]^\top\). State explicitly if your software stacks the blocks differently.

Validate at least one column with a central finite difference of the wrist position and one column with a small-angle rotation residual.

Exploit the spherical wrist

The last three joints do not translate their common intersection

For \(i=4,5,6\), the wrist point lies on axis \(i\). Therefore \(p_w-o_i\) is parallel to \(z_i\), and its cross product with \(z_i\) vanishes.

\[ J_w= \begin{bmatrix} J_{A,v}&0_{3\times3}\\ J_{A,\omega}&J_{W,\omega} \end{bmatrix} \]

Two mechanisms of rank loss

Arm singularity: joints 1–3 cannot move the wrist point in three independent directions.

Wrist singularity: the three wrist axes cannot create three independent angular velocities.

This zero block belongs to a Jacobian evaluated at the wrist point. Moving the operational point to the tool generally makes the wrist columns’ translational entries nonzero.

Determinant and rank

Factorization reveals which module becomes singular

\[ \det J_w(q)=\det J_{A,v}(q_1,q_2,q_3)\;\det J_{W,\omega}(q_4,q_5,q_6) \]
1

Write the complete symbolic or structured \(6\times6\) matrix before taking its determinant.

2

Factor the result and associate each factor with a column dependency.

3

Confirm zeros by rank or singular values, not only by floating-point determinant.

A determinant is available only because this task Jacobian is square. Its value has length scaling—here proportional to \(\mathrm{m}^3\)—so numerical thresholds change with units and with any translation/rotation row weighting. For non-square Jacobians, test rank or singular values.

Reduced position view

\(J_{A,v}\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\) explains wrist-position critical curves and arm-root multiplicity.

Full pose view

\(J_w\in\mathbb R^{6\times6}\) additionally includes wrist orientation singularities and flip-branch coalescence.

A two-dimensional plot is only a slice of the full singularity set. Joint limits and collisions can remove or disconnect configurations without making the kinematic determinant zero.
