Redundancy Resolution in Motion Planning

7R robot · one supplied workspace motion, many joint motions

Understand the null space, compare cost functions, and explore numerical and analytical strategies on the same rectangular path.

Show redundancy · one tool pose, different arm configurations

A 7R robot can reconfigure while keeping its tool pose fixed

\[\mathbf q_A\ne\mathbf q_B,\qquad f(\mathbf q_A)=f(\mathbf q_B)={}^0T_{t,d}\]
The same tool position and orientation admit a continuous family of nearby configurations at a regular pose. Along a supplied path \({}^0T_{t,d}(s)\), redundancy lets us choose how the arm moves internally.

Degrees of freedom · independent coordinates and motions

Seven joint degrees of freedom, six tool-pose degrees of freedom

What is a degree of freedom?

One independent coordinate needed to specify a configuration. This serial 7R robot has seven independent joint angles; its tool pose has three position and three orientation coordinates.

\[{}^0\mathcal V_t=\begin{bmatrix}{}^0\dot{\mathbf p}_t\\{}^0\boldsymbol\omega_t\end{bmatrix}={}^0J_t(\mathbf q)\dot{\mathbf q}\]

\({}^0J_t\in\mathbb R^{6\times7}\). The index \(t\) denotes the tool; the superscript \(0\) denotes the world frame.

Kernel = null space

\[\ker J_t=\{\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^7:J_t\mathbf v=\mathbf0\}\]

The kernel of a matrix is the set of vectors it maps to zero. For the Jacobian, these are joint velocities that produce zero instantaneous tool velocity.

One internal degree of freedom at full task rank

\[\dim\ker J_t=7-\operatorname{rank}J_t=7-6=1\]

At a regular pose, rank six means that all six tool-velocity components can be commanded locally. One independent joint-velocity direction remains for internal motion.

Joint limits restrict the usable configurations. At a singularity, task rank drops and nullity increases, while some tool-velocity directions become unavailable.

Build the geometric Jacobian

Every joint contributes a screw evaluated at the tool point

1 · Locate the axis

Multiply the URDF transforms along the chain to obtain a point \({}^{0}\mathbf p_i\) on joint axis \(i\) and its world-frame rotation \({}^{0}R_i\).

2 · Express it in the world frame

\[{}^{0}\mathbf a_i={}^0R_i\,{}^i\hat{\mathbf a}_i\]

Here frame \(i\) is attached to joint axis \(i\). Rotate the URDF axis into world coordinates using \({}^0R_i\).

3 · Take the cross product

\[{}^0J_{t,i}=\begin{bmatrix}{}^0\mathbf a_i\times({}^0\mathbf p_t-{}^0\mathbf p_i)\\{}^0\mathbf a_i\end{bmatrix}\]

Stack all seven columns in joint order.

\[{}^0J_t=\begin{bmatrix}{}^0J_{t,1}&{}^0J_{t,2}&\cdots&{}^0J_{t,7}\end{bmatrix}\]
We use angular velocity, not Euler-angle rates. The robot models and the analytical IK are checked against the same native KUKA iiwa 7 geometry.

From two configurations to a local null vector

Different joint velocities can produce the same desired tool velocity

1 · Take two configurations and two joint velocities

Let \(\mathbf q_A,\mathbf q_B\) realize the same tool pose, with respective velocities \(\dot{\mathbf q}_A,\dot{\mathbf q}_B\).

\[J_t(\mathbf q_A)\dot{\mathbf q}_A=\mathcal V_d,\quad J_t(\mathbf q_B)\dot{\mathbf q}_B=\mathcal V_d\]

The Jacobians generally differ. Their velocity difference is not automatically a null vector of either Jacobian.

What is \(\mathcal V_d\)?

\[\mathcal V_d\equiv{}^0\mathcal V_{t,d}=\begin{bmatrix}{}^0\dot{\mathbf p}_{t,d}\\{}^0\boldsymbol\omega_{t,d}\end{bmatrix}\]

The desired tool velocity: three linear and three angular components, expressed in the world frame. Holding the complete tool pose fixed means \(\mathcal V_d=\mathbf0\).

2 · Fix one configuration \(\mathbf q\)

At this same configuration, take two velocity solutions \(\dot{\mathbf q}_a,\dot{\mathbf q}_b\) of the same command.

\[J_t\dot{\mathbf q}_a=J_t\dot{\mathbf q}_b=\mathcal V_d\]\[J_t(\dot{\mathbf q}_b-\dot{\mathbf q}_a)=\mathbf0\]

3 · \(\mathbf n\) is a null vector

\[J_t\mathbf n=\mathbf0,\quad\|\mathbf n\|=1,\qquad\dot{\mathbf q}=\dot{\mathbf q}_p+\mathbf n\nu\]

\(\mathbf n\) is a nonzero direction in the kernel (null space). \(\dot{\mathbf q}_p\) is one velocity solution, and the scalar \(\nu\) sets the signed speed along the null direction.

At rank six, the 7R robot has one independent null direction. It usually coordinates several joints to preserve the commanded tool velocity.

Separate primary and secondary motion

The pseudoinverse follows the task; the projector selects internal motion

One particular solution

\[J^+=J^T(JJ^T)^{-1}\]

For rank six, \(J^+\mathcal V_d\) is the solution with minimum Euclidean joint-rate norm.

Project into the null space

\[N=I-J^+J\]

\(JN=0\), \(N^2=N\), and \(N^T=N\). For one redundant degree, \(N=\mathbf n\mathbf n^T\) if \(\|\mathbf n\|=1\).

All instantaneous solutions

\[\boxed{\dot{\mathbf q}=J^+\mathcal V_d+N\mathbf z}\]

Here \(J={}^0J_t(\mathbf q)\). The free vector \(\mathbf z\) expresses our secondary preference.

These identities describe the exact pseudoinverse. Damped least squares improves numerical conditioning, but its associated matrix is generally not an exact null-space projector.

The optimization problem · follow the task and improve the posture

Minimize a cost while preserving the prescribed tool pose

Choose a preferred configuration

\[\min_{\mathbf q}\ h(\mathbf q)\quad\text{subject to}\quad f(\mathbf q)={}^0T_{t,d}\]

\(h\) is a scalar cost: lower values mean a more desirable posture. The constraint keeps the commanded tool position and orientation. Joint limits must also be checked.

Take a local projected descent step

\[\boxed{\dot{\mathbf q}=J^+\mathcal V_d-\gamma N\nabla h},\qquad\gamma\ge0\]

\(J={}^0J_t\), \(N=I-J^+J\). The gradient \(\nabla h\) points toward increasing cost; \(-N\nabla h\) keeps its descent component in the null space. The gain \(\gamma\) sets the strength of this secondary motion.

Derive the change in cost

\[\dot h=\nabla h^T\dot{\mathbf q}\]\[=\nabla h^TJ^+\mathcal V_d-\gamma\|N\nabla h\|^2\]

The first line is the chain rule. Since \(N^T=N\) and \(N^2=N\), \(\nabla h^TN\nabla h=\|N\nabla h\|^2\).

Fixed tool pose versus a moving tool

With \(\mathcal V_d=\mathbf0\), \(\dot h=-\gamma\|N\nabla h\|^2\le0\): the cost decreases for \(\gamma>0\) whenever the projected gradient is nonzero.

With a moving tool, the primary-task term can increase the cost faster than the secondary motion reduces it.

Projected descent is a local optimization method. A zero projected gradient does not prove a global minimum, and the cost does not guarantee that joint limits can be avoided.

Choose a secondary cost

Choose one cost function h to express the secondary objective

Let \(q_{i,c}=(q_{i,\max}+q_{i,\min})/2\), \(r_i=(q_{i,\max}-q_{i,\min})/2\), and \(u_i=(q_i-q_{i,c})/r_i\). Each row defines a separate choice of \(h(\mathbf q)\). The reference posture is the selected starting IK.

Interpret \(u_i\): it is the joint’s dimensionless position relative to the centre of its allowed range. \(u_i=0\) is the centre, \(u_i=-1\) is the lower limit, and \(u_i=1\) is the upper limit. Thus \(|u_i|>1\) means the joint is outside its limits; this normalization lets us compare joints with different ranges.

Numerical path planning

Predict with the Jacobian, correct the pose, then check the step

1

Select one legal starting IK from the map and choose the null-space objective and its gain.

2

Compute the required tool displacement and the current Jacobian. Add the projected secondary motion to the pseudoinverse prediction.

3

Correct the finite-step position and orientation error. A first-order null prediction alone drifts away from a curved self-motion manifold.

4

Validate joint limits, full-Jacobian rank, pose accuracy, and continuity along the step. Stop and report the first failed check.

\[\mathbf q_{k+1}^{\mathrm{pred}}=\mathbf q_k+J_k^+\Delta\mathbf x_k-\gamma\Delta s\,N_k\nabla h(\mathbf q_k)\]
Here \(\Delta\mathbf x_k=[\Delta\mathbf p_k;\,\operatorname{Log}(R_{k+1}R_k^T)^\vee]\) is a small geometric pose increment, with both components expressed in the world frame.

Analytical IK for a redundant robot

Fixing the redundant angle turns a continuous family into discrete candidates

\[\mathcal M_s=\{\mathbf q:f(\mathbf q)={}^0T_{t,d}(s)\}\]
\[\mathcal S_s(\lambda)=\mathcal M_s\cap\{q_3=\lambda\}\]

Before fixing \(q_3\)

A reachable regular pose has a locally one-dimensional inverse image.

After fixing \(q_3=\lambda\)

The iiwa model generically has up to eight isolated analytical IK roots. A particular pose and angle can have fewer roots, and some roots violate joint limits.

“Eight IKs” refers to a fixed-angle slice. It does not mean that the redundant robot has only eight configurations for a tool pose.

How the analytical candidates are constructed

Solve the arm geometry, then the wrist orientation

1 · Two elbow choices

Let \(\mathbf w\) run from the shoulder to the wrist centre. The two arm lengths are \(a=b=0.4\) m.

\[c_4=\frac{\|\mathbf w\|^2-a^2-b^2}{2ab}\]
\[q_4=\pm\arccos c_4\]

2 · Two shoulder choices

With \(q_3=\lambda\), set \(A=a+bc_4\) and \(B=-bs_4\cos\lambda\).

\[w_z=A\cos q_2-B\sin q_2\]

This gives two generic \(q_2\) roots. Then use the horizontal wrist coordinates to recover \(q_1\).

3 · Two wrist choices

Remove the arm rotation from the desired tool rotation:

\[W=({}^0R_4)^T{}^0R_{t,d}\]

Resolve this spherical-wrist rotation into two generic \((q_5,q_6,q_7)\) solutions.

\[2\times2\times2=8\ \text{generic fixed-angle branches}\]
The three ± signs label fixed-angle algebraic roots; they are not global SEW classes for the redundant robot. Each root is checked by forward kinematics and native joint limits before planning its continuation.

Edges

Whole-path strategy

Analytical planning evaluates the global candidate structure. Numerical continuation resolves motion locally. A failed search on a finite grid does not prove that no continuous path exists.

A chart boundary is not always a robot singularity

Check the full 6 × 7 Jacobian before declaring a lost task direction

Fixed-angle chart

\[\bar J=J_{[:,\,1,2,4,5,6,7]}\in\mathbb R^{6\times6}\]

\(\det\bar J=0\) means that holding \(q_3\) fixed prevents local inversion of the remaining six joints. The robot may still have full task rank.

Full redundant robot

\[\operatorname{rank}J<6\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sigma_{\min}(\widehat J)=0\]

The full robot then loses an independent instantaneous task velocity. The redundant coordinate may need to change before this boundary is reached.

Concrete iiwa example: \(\mathbf q=(20,-35,30,50,-40,0,60)^\circ\) is within the joint limits. The full scaled Jacobian has \(\sigma_{\min}=0.0477\) m and rank six; removing the \(q_3\) column gives rank five. Here \(q_3\) fails as a local parameter while the tool retains all six instantaneous velocity directions.

The global idea

Redundancy resolution chooses a continuous route through the IK family

Understand the freedom

The null space identifies instantaneous internal motion that preserves the task velocity.

Choose a local preference

Check the global connections

The analytical path–angle maps expose alternative starts and redundancy schedules. Validate the entire connection before accepting a plan.

These demonstrations check kinematic tracking, native joint limits, and singularity margins. Collision checking, dynamics, and a physically smooth time law are additional requirements for execution on hardware.
